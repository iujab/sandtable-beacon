### Two-puck demo: what the twin does with two pucks (lane L16's evaluation, SIMULATED)

From `vendor/beacon/results/eval_two_puck.json` ("SIMULATED: the owner's two-puck demo: how often two pucks give one answer, and how often it is wrong"), made by lane L16's `scripts/eval_beacon.py` on its own simulator, not on phones or boards. Everything here is simulated.

Assumptions, as the file states them:

- What the phone gives: UWB ranges only (no los, rssi or fp fields: the iPhone gives a distance), no Bluetooth, no bearing, a still U1 phone, 3 s of ranges per puck at about 5 Hz; default noise model (body blocks, static link bias); twin on.
- Pucks: placed by hand at beacon.layout.recommend's spots: the locator gets those spots, the truth is within a few cm (N(0, 0.03 m) per axis).
- Phone positions: 400 phone positions uniform over the fire room (>= 0.3 m from walls) x 2 noise draws per layout (the reference: 1); false statement: 150 positions x 2 draws.
- Radio noise: 0.1 m per range in line of sight, a per-link bias of 0.1 m, +0.3 m through a wall, a body blocking the link 25% of the time; the locator assumes the same model.
- One point "by the twin": the locator (twin on) publishes a fix by itself. "After the occupant's statement": the locator publishes two candidates and beacon.prune.apply_statement with the occupant's stated suite (the fire room) leaves one: a fix that rests on what was said. "Two places": two (or more) candidates remain after the statement (both in the room, or none in it). A wrong point: a published fix whose error exceeds 3 sigma_m or 1.5 m.
